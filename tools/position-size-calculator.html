    Why position size matters more than entry

    Position sizing decides how much you lose when a trade goes wrong. The idea is simple: decide in advance the maximum amount of your account you are willing to lose on one trade, decide where your stop-loss goes based on the chart, and then let those two numbers set the trade size. The stop is placed where the trade idea is proven wrong; the size is adjusted so that being wrong costs only what you planned.

    The formula is: position size = money at risk ÷ (stop distance × value per pip per lot). With a $5,000 account and 1% risk, the money at risk is $50. On EUR/USD a standard lot moves about $10 per pip, so a 25-pip stop costs $250 per lot, and $50 ÷ $250 = 0.20 lots.

    Pip value for different instruments

    • Pairs quoted in USD (EUR/USD, GBP/USD, AUD/USD, NZD/USD): one pip is 0.0001, and for a 100,000-unit lot that is $10 per pip.
    • USD/JPY: one pip is 0.01 yen, so a lot moves 1,000 yen per pip. Converted to dollars that is 1,000 ÷ the current USD/JPY price.
    • USD/CHF, USD/CAD: one pip is 0.0001 of the quote currency, so the USD value is 10 ÷ the current price.
    • XAU/USD: conventions differ between brokers. This calculator assumes 100 oz per lot and a pip of $0.10, so a lot moves $10 per pip ($100 per $1 move in gold). Check your platform's contract specification.

    Mini (0.10) and micro (0.01) lots scale the pip value by the same factor. The calculator rounds down to 0.01 lot so the real loss never exceeds your limit.

    Using the table

    The risk table shows the same trade at several risk levels. It makes the trade-off visible: doubling the risk percentage doubles both the size and the loss at the stop. A sequence of losses at a high risk percentage can take a large share of an account, which is why many traders choose a small fixed percentage. Read more in How position sizing works.

    Frequently asked questions

    How do I calculate lot size from risk?

    Divide the money you are willing to lose by the stop-loss distance in pips multiplied by the pip value of one lot. For example, $50 ÷ (25 pips × $10) = 0.20 lots.

    What is the pip value of EUR/USD?

    For a standard lot of 100,000 euros, one pip (0.0001) is worth $10. A mini lot is $1 and a micro lot $0.10 per pip.

    How is a pip defined for gold?

    It varies by broker. Many use $0.01 or $0.10 as a pip for XAU/USD. This calculator uses $0.10 and 100 oz per lot; if your broker differs, choose 'Other' and enter its pip value per lot.

    Why does the calculator round down?

    Brokers usually accept sizes in steps of 0.01 lot. Rounding down keeps the loss at the stop at or below the amount you chose to risk.

    Are spreads and commissions included?

    No. They add to the cost of every trade, so on tight stops your real loss can be a little larger than shown.
