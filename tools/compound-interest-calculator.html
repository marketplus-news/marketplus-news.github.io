    What compound interest is

    Compound interest means you earn a return not only on the money you put in, but also on the returns you have already earned. In the first years most of the balance is your own deposits; over longer periods the growth line pulls away from the deposits line in the chart above, because each year's growth is calculated on a larger base. Time and consistency usually matter more than the size of any single deposit.

    How this calculator works

    It assumes deposits at the end of each month and monthly compounding at the annual rate ÷ 12. Each year you can raise the deposit by a percentage, for example to match pay rises. The today's money figure divides the ending balance by the inflation you enter for every year, which shows what the balance would buy at today's prices. That number is often a useful reality check on large long-term totals.

    The savings goal box works backwards with the standard future-value formula: given your starting amount, the return and the number of years, it finds the flat monthly deposit that reaches the target.

    Choosing a realistic return

    • Savings accounts and deposits pay a stated rate that changes with central bank rates; use the rate your bank actually offers.
    • Investments such as stock funds do not have a fixed return. Results vary from year to year and can be negative. Run the calculator with a cautious, a middle and an optimistic rate rather than relying on one number.
    • Fees and taxes reduce your real return. Subtract them from the rate for a more honest picture.

    If you carry high-interest debt, paying it off is often the best guaranteed "return" available. Compare with our debt payoff calculator.

    Frequently asked questions

    How often does interest compound in this calculator?

    Monthly, at the annual rate divided by 12, with deposits added at the end of each month. Accounts that compound daily give very slightly higher results.

    What return should I use?

    For a savings account, its current interest rate. For investments there is no guaranteed rate, so compare a few scenarios and treat the results as a range, not a forecast.

    What does 'today's money' mean?

    It is the ending balance adjusted for the inflation rate you entered, so you can see roughly what it would buy at today's prices.

    How is the monthly amount for a goal calculated?

    With the standard future-value-of-an-annuity formula: the target minus the grown starting amount, divided by the growth factor of a series of monthly deposits.

    Does the calculator store my numbers?

    Only in your own browser, so they are there next time. Nothing is sent anywhere.
