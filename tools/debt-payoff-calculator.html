    Payoff order

    #SnowballAvalanche

    Snowball and avalanche in one sentence each

    Both methods start the same way: you keep paying the minimum on every debt and put every spare dollar on top of one target debt. When that debt is gone, its minimum payment rolls into the next target, so your payment to the target grows over time. The only difference is the order.

    • Debt snowball: target the smallest balance first. You clear whole debts sooner, which many people find motivating.
    • Debt avalanche: target the highest interest rate first. Mathematically this usually costs the least interest and is never slower.

    How the calculator works

    Each month it adds interest to every balance (APR ÷ 12), pays each minimum, and sends the rest of your total monthly budget (the sum of your minimums plus the extra amount) to the target debt. When a debt reaches zero, the money that paid it automatically flows to the next target. It repeats this until every balance is zero and records the month each debt disappears.

    The minimums only column shows what happens if you never pay more than the required minimum on each debt and do not roll freed-up payments forward. It is usually the slowest and most expensive path, and if a minimum does not even cover the monthly interest, that balance never goes down.

    Which method should you choose?

    If the interest difference shown above is small, pick the method you are more likely to stick with; consistency matters more than the last few dollars. If the difference is large (typically when one high-rate card also has a large balance), the avalanche is worth considering. Some people use a hybrid: clear one tiny balance first for a quick win, then switch to avalanche order. We compare both in detail in Debt snowball vs avalanche, explained.

    The results are estimates for planning. Lenders may use daily interest, fees or promotional rates that change the numbers. This is not financial advice.

    Frequently asked questions

    Is the debt avalanche always cheaper than the snowball?

    With the same total monthly payment, ordering by highest interest rate first minimises interest in almost every case. When rates are similar, the two methods give nearly identical results, and the snowball may even finish a debt or two earlier.

    What should I enter as the extra payment?

    The amount you can reliably pay every month on top of all minimum payments. Even a small, steady extra amount shortens the payoff time, because it compounds through the rollover.

    Why does a debt say 'never'?

    Its minimum payment is lower than the interest charged each month, so the balance grows or stays flat. Increase the payment or contact the lender about the rate.

    Does it handle 0% promotional balances?

    Enter 0 as the APR. Keep in mind that when a promotion ends the rate changes, which this simple model does not track.

    Where are my debts stored?

    Only in your browser's local storage on this device. Nothing is sent to us, and the Reset button clears it.
