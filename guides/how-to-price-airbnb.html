    How to Price an Airbnb: A Step-by-Step Method

    Start from your costs, check the market, then adjust for seasons, days of the week and how far ahead guests book.

    Published November 22, 2026 · 7 min read

    Try it with your numbers: Airbnb & Short-Term Rental Profit Calculator (free)→

    Pricing a short-term rental is not about guessing the "right" number once. It is a system: a floor you never go below, a base rate grounded in what similar places charge, and adjustments for the times when demand is higher or lower. This guide walks through that system step by step.

    Step 1: Know your floor

    Before you look at a single competitor, work out the lowest average rate at which the listing still covers its costs. Add up your fixed monthly costs (rent or mortgage, utilities, internet, insurance, software) and your per-stay costs (cleaning, laundry, supplies), and take platform fees into account. Our short-term rental profit calculator shows this as the break-even nightly rate for a given occupancy.

    The floor is not your price. It is the line you only cross deliberately, for example to fill a gap night that would otherwise earn nothing.

    Step 2: Find real comparables

    Search the platform as a guest would, for your area and for specific future dates, and note listings that genuinely compete with yours: same type of place, similar number of guests and bedrooms, similar location and quality. Look at the total price a guest sees for a typical stay, including the cleaning fee, not only the headline nightly rate.

    • Check several date ranges: a weekday in a quiet month, a weekend, and a known busy period.
    • Pay attention to which listings are already booked for near dates; booked calendars tell you more than prices alone.
    • Note the reviews and ratings: a new listing with no reviews usually needs to start below established neighbours.

    Step 3: Set a base rate

    Your base rate is the price for an ordinary night: not a holiday, not a big event, not the dead of the low season. Place it within the range of your comparables according to how your listing stacks up. If you are new, a slightly lower rate can help win the first bookings and reviews; raise it once reviews arrive.

    Step 4: Adjust for demand

    • Seasons: raise prices in your high season and lower them in the low season. Your own booking history is the best guide once you have it.
    • Days of the week: city apartments and leisure destinations often have different busy days. Price the busy ones higher.
    • Events and holidays: concerts, conferences and public holidays can lift demand sharply for a few nights. Check local event calendars well ahead.
    • Lead time: if a date is close and still empty, a modest discount may fill it. If dates far in the future book unusually fast, your price may be too low.

    Step 5: Use minimum stays and fees on purpose

    Every stay means a turnover: cleaning, laundry and your time. A minimum stay of two or three nights reduces turnovers and can raise profit even if occupancy falls slightly. The cleaning fee also shapes demand: a high fee makes one-night stays look expensive, while folding part of the cleaning cost into the nightly rate makes short stays more competitive. Test both in the calculator to see the effect on monthly profit.

    Step 6: Review and repeat

    Set a regular time, for example every week, to look at the next 60 to 90 days. Are gaps forming? Are weekends booking months ahead? Adjust in small steps and note what happened. Dynamic pricing tools can automate much of this, but they still need a sensible base rate and floor from you.

    This guide is general information. Local rules, licences and taxes for short-term rentals vary widely; check them before you list.
