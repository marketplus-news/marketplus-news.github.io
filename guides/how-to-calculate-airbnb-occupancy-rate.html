    How to Calculate Airbnb Occupancy Rate, ADR and RevPAR

    Four numbers tell you how a short-term rental is really doing. Here is how to calculate each one and what to do with them.

    Published November 22, 2026 · 6 min read

    Try it with your numbers: Airbnb & Short-Term Rental Profit Calculator (free)→

    Hosts often judge a listing by how full the calendar looks. A full calendar at a low price can earn less than a half-empty one at a good price, so it helps to track a few standard numbers instead. This guide shows the formulas, a worked example and how the numbers fit together.

    Occupancy rate

    Occupancy rate = booked nights ÷ available nights × 100%.

    Count only nights the place was actually available to guests. Nights you blocked for yourself, for repairs or because of a minimum-stay rule you chose are usually left out of "available". Use the same rule every month, otherwise the trend means nothing.

    Example: in a 30-night month you blocked 2 nights for maintenance, so 28 nights were available. Guests booked 19 of them. Occupancy is 19 ÷ 28 = 67.9%.

    Average daily rate (ADR)

    ADR = room revenue ÷ booked nights.

    Room revenue here means what guests paid for the nights themselves. Many hosts keep cleaning fees out of ADR and track them separately, because they cover a cost rather than the stay. Decide once and stay consistent.

    Example: the 19 booked nights brought in $2,280 in nightly revenue. ADR is $2,280 ÷ 19 = $120.

    RevPAR: revenue per available night

    RevPAR = room revenue ÷ available nights, which is the same as ADR × occupancy rate.

    Example: $2,280 ÷ 28 = $81.43 (or $120 × 67.9%). RevPAR is the most useful single number for pricing decisions, because it rewards the balance between price and occupancy. Raising the rate is only a win if RevPAR goes up too.

    In this illustration the cheaper month earns the most per available night, but it also means 24 turnovers' worth of guests, against 15 at the higher price. That is why profit, not only revenue, decides which option is better.

    Break-even occupancy

    Break-even occupancy is the share of nights you must book just to cover your costs. Roughly:

    Break-even occupancy = fixed monthly costs ÷ (available nights × (ADR − variable cost per night))

    Variable cost per night includes platform fees and your cleaning and supplies spread over the nights of an average stay. Example: fixed costs of $1,400 a month, ADR $120, platform fee 15% ($18) and $22 of cleaning and supplies per night gives $80 left per booked night. $1,400 ÷ (28 × $80) = 62.5%. Below that occupancy the month loses money at this price.

    The Airbnb profit and occupancy calculator does this with your own numbers, including cleaning fees per stay and the break-even nightly rate.

    How to use these numbers

    • Track monthly and compare the same month year on year. Seasonality makes month-to-month comparisons misleading.
    • Watch RevPAR when you change prices. If occupancy drops but RevPAR rises, the change probably worked; check profit too.
    • Keep break-even occupancy well below your typical occupancy. The gap is your safety margin for quiet months.
    • Use your own data first. Market reports are useful context, but your listing's history is the best guide. See how to price an Airbnb for setting the rates themselves.

    The figures above are a worked illustration, not market averages. Local rules, licences and taxes for short-term rentals vary; check them before you list.
