    Hyrox Pacing Strategy: Runs, Stations and Roxzone

    Even pacing, controlled first kilometres and a plan for the sleds and wall balls: how to avoid the most common Hyrox mistake.

    Published November 22, 2026 · 6 min read

    Try it with your numbers: Hyrox Finish Time Calculator (free)→

    Hyrox combines eight 1 km runs with eight workout stations, so it is closer to an endurance race than to a gym workout. The most common mistake is the same one runners make in a marathon: starting too fast. Because the stations fatigue the legs and grip, energy spent too early is paid back with interest later. A pacing plan helps you finish strong.

    Know the order

    The order never changes: SkiErg, Sled Push, Sled Pull, Burpee Broad Jumps, Rowing, Farmers Carry, Sandbag Lunges and Wall Balls, each preceded by a 1 km run. The second half is leg-heavy: lunges and wall balls come when you are most tired, so a plan that leaves something in the tank for the last two stations usually pays off.

    Pace the runs by effort, not ego

    • Run 1 feels easy because you are fresh and surrounded by other athletes. Hold back to your planned pace; it is the cheapest place to lose a race.
    • Runs 2 to 4 come after the SkiErg and the sleds. Expect your legs to feel heavy for the first few hundred metres and settle into rhythm rather than sprinting to recover lost time.
    • Runs 5 to 8 are where good pacing shows. If you held back early, you can keep a steady pace while others slow down.

    Many athletes find that their run splits get slower over the race even when their effort feels constant. The Hyrox finish time calculator lets you model this with its run fade setting.

    Station strategy

    • SkiErg and Rowing: steady, repeatable strokes at a pace you could hold a little longer. Going all out on the erg spikes your heart rate before the next run.
    • Sled Push and Pull: these punish poor technique more than lack of fitness. Low body position on the push, and a consistent hand-over-hand or walking pattern on the pull, keep you moving with fewer stops.
    • Burpee Broad Jumps: find a rhythm you can keep without long pauses; many short rests cost more time than a slightly slower continuous pace.
    • Farmers Carry: grip is the limiter. Fast, short steps and as few put-downs as possible.
    • Sandbag Lunges and Wall Balls: plan your sets before the race (for example, wall balls in fixed sets with short planned breaks) so tired decisions are not made on the spot.

    Do not ignore the Roxzone

    Transition time counts. Walk or jog purposefully through the Roxzone, know where each station is from the course map, and avoid long breaks before you start a station. Seconds saved in each of sixteen transitions add up.

    Use a split sheet

    Write your target elapsed time after each segment on a band or card. If you are well ahead early, ease off; if you are behind after the second half starts, do not panic, because most athletes slow down there too. Build your own sheet with the calculator by entering your training times; its split table gives the elapsed time after every run and station.
