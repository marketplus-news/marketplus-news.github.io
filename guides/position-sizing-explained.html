    How Position Sizing Works in Forex and Gold

    Risk a planned amount, let the stop-loss set the distance, and let the size follow. The maths behind every trade size.

    Published November 22, 2026 · 6 min read

    Try it with your numbers: Position Size & Pip Value Calculator (free)→

    Educational content only. Leveraged trading in forex and gold carries a high risk of loss. Nothing here is a recommendation to trade.

    Two traders can take the same trade, at the same price, with the same stop, and have completely different outcomes, because one traded ten times the size. Position sizing is the part of trading that decides how much a loss actually costs. It is also the part you fully control.

    The three inputs

    1. Money at risk: how much of the account you accept to lose if the trade fails. A common approach is a fixed percentage of the current balance.
    2. Stop-loss distance: how far the price must move against you, in pips or price units, before the trade idea is wrong. It comes from the chart, not from the account size.
    3. Value per pip: how much one pip is worth for one lot of the instrument.

    Put together: lots = money at risk ÷ (stop distance in pips × pip value per lot).

    Example: EUR/USD

    A $5,000 account risking 1% has $50 at risk. The stop is 25 pips away. On a standard lot (100,000 euros) one pip, 0.0001, is worth $10, so 25 pips cost $250 per lot. $50 ÷ $250 = 0.20 lots, or two mini lots.

    Example: USD/JPY

    For USD/JPY one pip is 0.01 yen, so a standard lot of $100,000 moves 1,000 yen per pip. To convert to dollars, divide by the exchange rate: at 150.00, 1,000 ÷ 150 ≈ $6.67 per pip. With the same $50 risk and a 25-pip stop: $50 ÷ (25 × $6.67) ≈ 0.30 lots. The pip value changes as the rate moves, so recalculate rather than reuse an old number.

    Example: XAU/USD

    Gold contracts are commonly 100 ounces per lot, so a $1 move in gold is $100 per lot. Brokers define the "pip" differently ($0.01 or $0.10), which is a frequent source of confusion. Think in dollars of price movement instead: a $5 stop on one lot is $500. With $50 at risk, $50 ÷ $500 = 0.10 lots. Always confirm the contract size in your platform's specification.

    Why a fixed percentage?

    Risking a fixed percentage of the current balance means positions shrink automatically after losses and grow after gains. It also makes losing streaks survivable: every trader has them, and the size of each loss determines how deep the drawdown gets. Choosing the percentage is a personal decision; the point is to choose it before the trade, not during it.

    Common mistakes

    • Moving the stop to fit a size. The stop belongs where the idea is invalid; adjust the size instead.
    • Ignoring the pip value. The same lot size risks different amounts on EUR/USD, USD/JPY and gold.
    • Forgetting costs. Spreads, commissions and slippage add to every loss, especially with tight stops.
    • Not recording trades. A journal with planned risk and actual result shows whether your sizing rules are being followed.
